Signing seven institutions, including four IITs, by going through students first

Most startups email the director and wait. I started with the student leaders who actually run campus life, and let them open the doors.

The setup

Trumio's first product was a marketplace. Companies posted small, real projects. Students bid to do them, and the winning bid did the work. Companies got work done quickly and affordably. Students got paid, and got real projects for their résumés.

Our sales leaders handled the companies. I owned the other side: getting strong engineering students onto the platform, starting with India's top engineering institutes, the IITs and NITs.

The approach: bottom up, not top down

The obvious route is to email the director and wait for a reply that never comes. But IITs have a strong culture of student-led administration. General secretaries, Gymkhana presidents and club heads run much of campus life, and they have the ear of the deans. I didn't need everyone. I needed one or two of them to trust us.

  1. Start with the leverage we already hadOur CEO and CTO are both IIT Kharagpur alumni. Students want to hear from alumni and experienced people, so I led with that.
  2. Find the people who run the campusI went through each institute's website to find the current general secretaries and club leaders, then reached out on LinkedIn and email.
  3. Ask for five minutes, not a partnershipA short intro: who I am, what we're building. Most students happily took the call. In those five minutes I was direct about what we wanted, and asked for a half-hour.
  4. Bring in the foundersThat half-hour was with our CEO or CTO and the student body. Alumni talking to students is a conversation IITians welcome.
  5. Pay to get in the room when neededWhere the no-cost route stalled, we sponsored a student festival, and asked in return for a meeting with the dean responsible, such as the Dean of Academic Affairs.
  6. Sign the agreementA non-monetary agreement: the institute lets students work on real projects through Trumio, and we bring them opportunities through our clients.1 to 3 months per institute

Being there, not just emailing

None of this worked from a laptop alone. I spent days and nights on campus: talking to students in their hostels, eating dinner with them, helping them with career questions, and learning what actually drove them. That trust is what turned a five-minute call into an introduction to the dean.

What students actually needed

Talking to thousands of students, especially third- and fourth-years, changed our pitch. With placements this competitive, they needed real projects, and most didn't have them.

What they had

Projects recycled from their seniors, repeated year after year. Nothing live, nothing a company could see working in the real world.

What we pitched

Real work for real companies. Earn money, see where your skills actually stand, build a portfolio, and build relationships with the companies themselves.

That reframing is what got them to sign up.

The results

The drop from students to projects was a demand problem, not a supply one. The student side I owned outran the projects coming in from companies. And the projects that did run worked: 1 in 5 clients came back for more, often rehiring the same student team, which gave students repeat work, income and real relationships.

Signed agreements with seven institutions:

IIT Kharagpur IIT Kanpur IIT BHU IIT Bhubaneswar KIIT Manipal Institute of Technology SRCC

What didn't work

Where students dropped off

Waiting for a first project

Many expected work straight after signing up. Matching took time.

Bids not accepted

Some bids were priced too high, or companies didn't see the right fit for the project.

No time to commit

A project took four to five weeks, and these students are busy all year.

Those who did win projects usually saw them through end to end.

The campus that said no

One top IIT turned us down. It was confident in its own ways of sourcing projects and its own industry relationships. Our playbook didn't work there either: student leaders couldn't reach the academic leadership the way they could at, say, IIT Kanpur. We tried going straight to the top, and that didn't work either. Not every door opens the same way, and a channel that works on ten campuses can fail on the eleventh.

Where it led

The marketplace was later shut down when Trumio pivoted, but this work shaped what came next. If students at the IITs, the best in the country, were struggling to find real projects, while hiring slowed and company expectations rose, then students at tier 2 and tier 3 colleges were in a far harder spot. And the large IT services firms that hire them by the tens of thousands needed those engineers to arrive with real, practical experience.

That gap became the opportunity behind Trumio Enterprise and Trumio Pro, and eventually Mio.

What I'd take from it

Start with the leverage you already have.

Two IIT Kharagpur alumni on the founding team opened more doors than any cold email could.

Go where decisions start, not where they end.

Deans signed the agreements, but student leaders decided whether we'd get the meeting.

Ask for five minutes, not a partnership.

A small, specific ask gets a yes. The bigger conversation follows.

Sell what people are missing.

Students didn't want "a marketplace." They wanted to stop recycling their seniors' projects.

The hardest customers tell you where the market is.

If the best students were struggling, everyone else was too. That insight shaped Trumio's pivot.

Relationships, built one conversation at a time, opened doors that a budget alone couldn't.
