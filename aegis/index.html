Catching UPI fraud before the money leaves.

Aegis is a fraud model trained on simulated UPI payments covering six real scam patterns. It scores each payment in milliseconds, explains every decision in plain language, and lets a product team choose how much fraud to stop against how many genuine customers to interrupt.

How it's built

From simulated payments to a model you can question

Real UPI data isn't public, so the first job was building a realistic simulation of it. Everything else follows how a fraud team at a payments company would work.

    What went wrong firstThe first version scored 99.7% on every metric. That wasn't a win: the simulator made every fraudster's account brand new and every genuine one old, so the model just learned "new account means fraud". I added genuine newcomers and newly opened shops, rented and dormant mule accounts, fake merchant QR codes, an impersonation scam that looks like a normal payment, and unreported fraud. That brought the logistic regression down to the range you'd expect, and showed where gradient boosting really earns its place.
    Built by Arabinda Narayan Das. The payments are simulated; no real customer data was used. Model: LightGBM, explanations: path contributions computed from the trees, text model: character n-gram logistic regression.
