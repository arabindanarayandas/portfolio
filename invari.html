Invari: fixing the gap between what AI agents send and what APIs expect

An AI assistant told a customer their table was booked. When they arrived, there was no table. Invari is a side project built to stop that kind of silent failure.

The booking that never happened

A customer asks a restaurant's AI assistant: "Book me a table for four this Friday at 7pm." The assistant understands perfectly, takes the action, and replies: "Done! Your table is booked." The customer arrives on Friday. There is no table.

Here's what happened in between. The AI sent the booking system a request the system couldn't accept, and the error never reached the customer. Three parties, three different realities:

The AI assistant
"Done. Your table is booked."
The booking system
400 Bad Request
The customer
"See you at 7."

The problem: AI speaks in words, systems speak in rules

AI agents produce human-shaped values. The systems they call expect strict formats. Telling the AI to "be careful with formats" helps sometimes, but it's a request, not a guarantee.

"four"4

"£250.00"25000 (in pence)

"07700900234""+447700900234"

How it works

    
Invari sits between the AI agent and the system it calls. Every request passes through it first. It checks the request against the system's published rules (its OpenAPI spec), repairs what it safely can, blocks what it can't, and forwards a clean request. Setup is a single change:

    

      
BEFORE
BASE_URL = "https://api.yourapp.com/v1"

      
AFTER
BASE_URL = "https://proxy.invari.ai/agents/ag-7f3a9b2c/v1"

    

    
The agent doesn't know anything changed. Neither does the system. No SDK, no new code, and it works with any AI model or agent framework.

    

    
The product calls I made

    

      

        
Fix with rules, not another AI.

        
If a second AI repaired the first AI's mistakes, the same kind of error could come back. Invari's repairs are fixed rules: the same input always gives the same output, and every fix can be explained.

      

      

        
Judge a request by its weakest fix.

        
Each repair gets a confidence score from 0 to 1. The request's score is the lowest of them, so one shaky fix can't hide behind several good ones.

      

      

        
When in doubt, block. Never guess.

        
"A few hundred pounds" isn't a payment amount. Invari repairs obvious format errors and refuses to invent missing meaning. Blocked requests come back with a clear reason.

      

      

        
The higher the stakes, the higher the bar.

        
Default thresholds rise with the cost of a wrong fix:

        

          
0.80General

          
0.92Finance and insurance

          
0.95Healthcare

        

      

      

        
Show teams what their AI actually sends.

        
Most teams have never seen the raw requests their agent makes. Every request is logged with the original, the repaired version and each fix applied.

What I learned

    

      

        
The failure is usually in the request, not the thinking.

        
AI agents rarely misunderstand the customer. They mis-format what they send. Fixing the format fixes a surprising share of "AI failures."

      

      

        
Saying no is a feature.

        
The most important thing Invari does is refuse to guess. Trust comes from what a product won't do.

      

      

        
Lead with the story, not the spec.

        
"There was no table" explains Invari faster than any diagram.

      

      

        
Don't publish a number you haven't measured.

        
An early draft quoted a failure rate nobody had measured. We took it out everywhere. Real numbers will come from real traffic.
